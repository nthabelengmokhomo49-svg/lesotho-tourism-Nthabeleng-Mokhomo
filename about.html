About Lesotho

The Kingdom in the Sky – history, culture and people

A Unique African Kingdom

Lesotho is a landlocked country completely surrounded by South Africa. It is the only independent state in the world that lies entirely above 1,000 metres in elevation – earning it the nickname “the Kingdom in the Sky”.

The country is home to the Basotho people, known for their distinctive blankets, conical straw hats (mokorotlo) and strong highland traditions. The official languages are Sesotho and English.

From snow-capped peaks in winter to rolling green valleys in summer, Lesotho offers a climate and landscape unlike anywhere else in Africa.

History & Culture

In the early 19th century, King Moshoeshoe I united various clans to form the Basotho nation. He established his stronghold at Thaba Bosiu (“Mountain of Night”), a natural fortress that protected the people during the Difaqane wars. Lesotho became a British protectorate in 1868 and gained full independence on 4 October 1966.

The Basotho blanket (Seanamarena) is an iconic part of national dress, worn for warmth and ceremony. The mokorotlo (conical hat) appears on the national flag and is a symbol of Lesotho’s identity. Traditional music, dance and storytelling remain vibrant in rural communities.

Lesotho’s lowest point is about 1,400 m above sea level. The highest peak, Thabana Ntlenyana, rises to 3,482 m. Winters (May–August) can bring snow to the highlands, while summers are mild and ideal for hiking. The country is the source of major rivers that supply water to South Africa via the Lesotho Highlands Water Project.

Popular experiences include 4×4 trips over Sani Pass, pony trekking in the highlands, visiting Maletsunyane Falls, exploring Katse Dam, cultural stays in Basotho villages, and winter skiing at Afriski. Lesotho is also known for its fossil-rich sandstone caves and dinosaur footprints.

Traveller Tip

Pack layers – highland weather can change quickly. A warm jacket is essential even in summer evenings, and winter visitors should prepare for snow on the higher passes.
